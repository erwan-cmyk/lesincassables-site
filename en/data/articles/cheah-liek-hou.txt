On the court at Yoyogi National Stadium, in Tokyo, on September 4, 2021, Cheah Liek Hou wraps up a match unlike any other. Against Indonesia's Dheva Anrimusthi, the very man who had beaten him in the final of the World Championships in Basel two years earlier, the Malaysian wins 21-17, 21-15. That day, para badminton makes its debut on the program of the Paralympic Games, and Cheah Liek Hou becomes its very first Paralympic champion in history.

"I feel really great," he says after the medal ceremony. "I released all of my stress and tension, I released everything just now. I tried my best. I was really focused on the match and I deserved the win today." He adds that there was definitely pressure on his team, because everyone was looking to him and hoping for a good result at these Games, and dedicates his medal to all of Malaysia's badminton lovers.

Cheah Liek Hou is born on March 8, 1988, in Kuala Lumpur. Shortly after his birth, a nerve in his right shoulder is damaged — an obstetric brachial plexus palsy, sometimes called Erb's palsy, which will permanently reduce the strength and stability of his right hand. Yet nothing in the childhood that follows looks like an obstacle. A hyperactive kid from Kuala Lumpur, he spends his time outdoors, between table tennis and badminton. At nine, he picks up a racket on the courts of his school, with no ambition other than having fun.

At twelve, he wins gold at the Kuala Lumpur state level in both table tennis and badminton, and has to choose a discipline. His mother, who spotted his ease on a court very early, steers him toward badminton, Malaysia's national sport. Cheah enters the Bukit Jalil Sports School, joins the able-bodied national team at twelve, then the national junior team at fifteen. In 2003, he is invited to play a para badminton tournament. Two years later, in 2005, he wins his first world titles, in singles and doubles, in the SU5 class, reserved for players with an upper-limb impairment.

But para badminton at the time remains a sport without Paralympic Games, and in 2015 Cheah seriously considers hanging up his racket. In the meantime, he has earned a degree in corporate communication from Universiti Putra Malaysia and taken a job as a strategic account manager at a company in the sports sector, a well-paid position he then imagines holding for the long term. The turning point comes with the announcement that para badminton will be added to the program of the Tokyo 2020 Games: Cheah resigns to devote himself entirely to training, loses some twenty kilos ahead of the competition, and accepts both the drop in income and the sometimes skeptical looks directed, past thirty, at an athlete no longer of a beginner's age.

That bet, he wins in Tokyo. The gold medal also makes him the first Malaysian Paralympic medalist in the history of para badminton: a double first, for a sport that has only just entered the Games and for a country that had never before won a medal in the discipline — an outcome Cheah surely did not imagine in 2015, when he was thinking of giving it all up.

Three years later, at the Porte de La Chapelle Arena, he defends his title. On September 3, 2024, he beats Indonesia's Suryo Nugroho 21-13, 21-15 in forty-two minutes, his fourteenth win in eighteen meetings with this same rival, and hands Malaysia its first gold medal of the Paris Paralympic Games. On the court, he embraces his coach before holding up the Malaysian flag.

His dominance is not limited to the Games. In February 2026, in Bahrain, he completes a double at the World Championships: a ninth singles title against Taiwan's Fang Jen Yu, then a seventh doubles title alongside Mohamad Faris Ahmad Azri, a first world crown for the latter. Sixteen world titles in all, collected since 2005. "This could be my last World Championships," he tells the BWF at the time. "Maybe because of my age factor."

In 2025, Malaysia had honored him by naming him an Officer of the Order of the Defender of the Realm, a year after the release of Gold, the film directed by Adrian Teh and inspired by his trajectory. Married to Dewi Febriana Tan, Cheah Liek Hou nonetheless says he is considering holding on until the Los Angeles 2028 Games, if his health allows.
