On September 2, 2012, under the lights of the ExCeL Arena in London, Fatma Omar lies down on the bench for her third attempt. She is 38 years old, stands 1.45 meters tall, and is wearing Egypt's colors on a Paralympic podium for the fourth consecutive time. The bar is loaded to 142 kilos. She lowers it to her chest, then pushes it back up to full extension until her elbows lock: a new world and Paralympic record. A few minutes later, outside the competition, she attempts and makes one more lift, at 143 kilos, for the sheer pleasure of pushing the limit further still.

"I am so happy and thrilled to win the gold medal. I am glad I lifted Egypt's name high today," she says that day, in remarks relayed by the International Paralympic Committee.

This scene crowns a trajectory begun nearly forty years earlier in Cairo, where Fatma Omar is born in 1973. At the age of one, she contracts poliomyelitis, a disease that leaves lasting damage to her spine. She grows up at a time when women's Paralympic sport remains, in Egypt as elsewhere, little developed. It is in powerlifting that she finds her path: a discipline performed entirely lying on a bench, in which the athlete lowers the bar to the chest before pushing it back up to full arm extension, and which is reserved for athletes whose impairment affects the lower limbs or hips. Here, only upper-body strength counts.

She first makes her mark far from the Games: in 1998, in Dubai, she becomes world champion in the under-44-kilo category. Two years later, in Sydney, she discovers the Paralympic Games and wins gold right away, with a lift of 109 kilos that beats Nigeria's Lucy Ejike — a rival who will mark the rest of her career, right up to her very last podiums.

In 2004, in Athens, she moves up a category, to the under-56 kilos, and dominates the event with a lift of 127.5 kilos. Four years later, in Beijing, she raises her record to 141.5 kilos, a new world record that earns her a third consecutive Paralympic title. Then comes London, in 2012, and that 142-kilo lift that will remain one of the peaks of her career.

Four Games, four gold medals, in two different weight categories, over twelve years of competition: such consistency is rare in Paralympic sport, where elite careers seldom stretch beyond a decade.

The streak ends in Rio, in 2016. Fatma Omar is 42 and now competes in the under-61 kilos. Against her, Lucy Ejike, still there sixteen years after their first duel in Sydney, sets a new world record at 142 kilos. Fatma Omar falls just short, with a lift of 140 kilos, and has to settle, for the first time in her Paralympic career, for silver. It is nonetheless her fifth medal at the Games, won at her fifth consecutive appearance since Sydney — a record the defeat does not tarnish.

Yet she does not stop there. In 2019, in Nur-Sultan, she climbs back onto a world championship podium and takes bronze, proof that past 45, she remains competitive against far younger opponents. Two years later, in August 2021, in Tokyo, at 47, she is chosen to carry the Egyptian flag at the opening ceremony, alongside the rest of the delegation. On the platform, in yet another new category, the under-67 kilos, she lifts 120 kilos and leaves with a second consecutive silver medal, behind China's Tan Yujiao. It is her sixth Paralympic medal, in six appearances, from Sydney 2000 to Tokyo 2020: twenty-one years at the top level without interruption. A few months later, in December 2021, in Tbilisi, she steps onto a world championship podium one last time and takes bronze, at 48.

A mother of two daughters, Fatma Omar will thus have gone through six editions of the Paralympic Games, in four different weight categories, without ever leaving the top of the world rankings. From one generation of rivals to the next, from one host city to the next, she will have tirelessly repeated the same movement: load the bar, lower it to the chest, then push it back up a little higher — and with it, the name of Egypt.
