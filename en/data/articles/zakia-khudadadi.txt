Under the glass roof of the Grand Palais, on August 29, 2024, Zakia Khudadadi raises both arms after beating Turkey's Nurcihan Ekinci 9 points to 1. She wears the jersey of the Refugee Paralympic Team, a delegation created to give a sporting existence to athletes forcibly displaced around the world, and one that had never, until then, brought home a single medal from a Paralympic competition. By taking bronze in para taekwondo, in the K44 -47 kg category, the 25-year-old Afghan writes the first line of that record.

Three years earlier, she did not know whether she would get out of Kabul alive.

Zakia Khudadadi is born on September 29, 1998, in Herat Province, Afghanistan, into a Hazara family. She grows up with a left arm that has been less functional than the other since birth, a difference that, in the conservative society around her, exposes her early to the gaze and judgment of others: for years, she hides her arm under a large scarf. It is sport that turns this equation around. At nine, a mentor who is himself disabled steers her toward taekwondo, a discipline she can practice with minimal equipment; he will train her for ten years, often in the family garden for lack of access to a club. She nonetheless pursues higher education in Herat while training. In 2016, at eighteen, she wins the African para taekwondo championship, held in Egypt — her first major victory, which qualifies her for the Tokyo Paralympic Games.

She will get there only at the last moment. In mid-August 2021, the Taliban retake Kabul. The airports close, the Afghan Paralympic Committee announces that its athletes will not be able to travel to Japan, and Khudadadi finds herself stranded in the capital, separated from her family back in Herat. A woman, disabled and a member of the Shia Hazara minority, she understands what the Taliban victory means for her. She records a video appeal in which she says she is not losing hope and asks the international community not to abandon Afghan women to their fate. The message spreads, relayed in particular by the club France-Afghanistan, and sets off an international mobilization. On August 28, 2021, she is evacuated from Kabul with her teammate Hossain Rasouli and some fifty other Afghan athletes, as part of an operation organized by the Australian authorities. Hosted for a week at a training center of the French sports ministry, she reaches Tokyo just in time: she becomes the first Afghan woman to compete at a Paralympic Games in seventeen years, eliminated in the early rounds of the K44 -49 kg category by Uzbekistan's Ziyodakhon Isakova, then in the repechage.

Returning to Afghanistan is no longer an option: the Taliban now bar women from playing sports and from education. Her family spends nearly two years in a reception center for asylum seekers before settling in France, while the administrative and practical hurdles are worked out. Khudadadi, for her part, moves into the Institut national du sport, de l'expertise et de la performance (Insep), France's national sports institute, where she trains under Haby Niaré, a former taekwondo world champion. In 2023, she wins the European para taekwondo title in Munich. Three years of work that lead her, in 2024, to the mat of the Grand Palais.

That day, she first beats Cuba's Lilisbet Rodríguez Rivero 21 to 11, before losing in the quarterfinal to Isakova, already her opponent in Tokyo and now the silver medalist. Given a second chance through the repechage, she faces Turkey's Nurcihan Ekinci, beats her 9 to 1 by snatching several points in the final minute, and takes the bronze. "This medal is for all the women of Afghanistan and all the refugees of the world," she declares after the bout, before adding that she has won because behind her there is the French Republic, the refugee team, and her best coach, Haby Niaré. She also says she wants to give this medal to the whole world, hoping that one day freedom will return to her country. The United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees, Filippo Grandi, hails a victory that embodies, in his view, the young woman's strength and determination — and one that resonates on behalf of the tens of millions of people forcibly displaced around the world.

A French citizen since October 28, 2025, Zakia Khudadadi continues to carry onto the mat the two stories that now make up her own. European champion again in 2026, she is preparing at Insep for the Los Angeles Paralympic Games, in 2028, where she is aiming for gold — in the discipline a coach once chose for her, convinced she belonged there.
